## Opening draft

A piece of equipment can solve the process problem on paper and still be useless if it does not fit inside the plant.

That is the kind of problem I deal with. I am a chemical engineer working in mineral processing and capital-equipment sales. My job is to turn a client's process problem into a technical solution, a price and a proposal.

Preparing those proposals involves a lot of repeated work. That is why I started building small tools of my own.

I want to show you what that looks like through a typical equipment request.

## Plain-language equipment explanations

**Cyclones:** A cyclone uses pressure and swirling motion to separate particles by size, or to remove water from a mixture of solids and water.

**Spirals:** A spiral uses gravity and flowing water to separate heavier valuable minerals from lighter waste as the material travels down a curved channel.

**Sampling equipment:** Sampling equipment takes a small but representative portion of a larger material stream so the plant can test what is moving through the process.

## Outline

### Where the role fits

- Explain that mining removes the rock and the process plant turns it into a useful or concentrated product.
- Explain that I work for an equipment supplier.
- Introduce sampling equipment, cyclones and spirals without teaching the full process.

### What capital sales means

- The client does not select a fixed item from a catalogue.
- We need to understand the process duty, choose or size equipment and prepare a technical and commercial proposal.
- An operator may need equipment soon for an existing plant.
- An engineering company may be working on an early study or a detailed design. A purchase may still be far away.

### Fictional sampler RFQ

An existing mineral-processing plant wants to test the particle size of material moving through its process. It needs a representative sample.

The client sends an RFQ with some process information, but the plant was not designed with a sampler in mind. The documents do not show whether enough space exists, whether operators can access the equipment safely or what plant changes may be required.

We review the process information and drawings, ask questions and visit the plant. On site, we measure possible installation locations and check access and maintenance space.

Several locations may be possible, but each has tradeoffs. One may require structural changes. Another may be hard to maintain. Another may not produce a representative sample.

We select the most practical arrangement, size the equipment, identify other work the plant may need, prepare the price and submit the proposal. If the client places the order, a project manager takes over delivery.

Clearly label this as a fictional example based on common work.

### Composite installation problem

- Base the example on common situations, not one identifiable project.
- Sampling was not included in the fictional plant layout, so there is no obvious installation point.
- Show several possible locations on an invented drawing.
- Explain the checks for sample quality, physical fit, blockages and maintenance access.
- Describe proposal outcomes in general. Do not connect the example to a real pending proposal.

### What most days look like

- Most time goes into meetings, proposals, process information and clarifications.
- Site visits and conferences are a smaller part of the calendar.
- The limits of the role are sometimes unclear. A large part of the work is finding the right person and getting the missing answer.

### Why I build tools

- Proposal documents and pricing include many repeated steps.
- I have coded for about eight years and have built tools at previous jobs and for personal use.
- Past projects include a personal finance app and a mineral-processing software project.
- I build small tools to fix repeated problems. They do not need to become polished software products.

Possible spoken transition:

> A lot of the engineering is specific to the equipment, but the proposal process repeats. You review documents, collect missing information, prepare calculations, coordinate pricing and build another Word proposal.
>
> I have been coding for about eight years, so when I see the same manual steps happening again and again, I usually start wondering whether I can make them easier.
>
> I am not trying to build perfect commercial software. Most of these tools begin as something small that fixes one annoying part of my own work. Sometimes the idea works. Sometimes I rebuild it several times. That process will be another part of this channel.

### Working from home and family

Keep this to one short section:

> I work from home most of the time. That gives me long stretches to focus on proposals, and avoiding the commute gives me more flexibility around family life. Site visits and conferences interrupt that routine, which is something I want to show honestly on this channel.
